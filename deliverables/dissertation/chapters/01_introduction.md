# Chapter 1: Introduction

## 1.1 Chapter Introduction

This chapter defines the Business Analytics problem: climate-sensitive listed companies increasingly report climate risks, but the financial specificity of those disclosures remains uneven. It establishes the regulatory context, research gap, approved aim, objectives, questions, scope and contribution. Method and results are reserved for later chapters.

## 1.2 Background and Regulatory Context

Climate-related reporting has moved from voluntary sustainability narrative towards information intended to support capital allocation and financial decision-making. The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) established a widely adopted architecture around governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures, 2017). The International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) subsequently issued IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures, which requires information about both current and anticipated effects of climate-related risks and opportunities on financial position, financial performance and cash flows, together with the way those matters enter financial planning (IFRS Foundation, 2023).

The UK Government finalised United Kingdom Sustainability Reporting Standards (UK SRS) S1 and S2 on 25 February 2026, adapting the ISSB baseline for the UK. On 30 September 2026, the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) confirmed a comply-or-explain regime for listed companies for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027 (Department for Business and Trade, 2026; Financial Conduct Authority, 2026). The annual reports used in this study therefore form a pre-implementation baseline: the evidence was limited to reports publicly available by 31 August 2026, before the new listing rules became operative.

## 1.3 Research Problem and Rationale

The problem is incomplete financial connectivity rather than absent climate disclosure. Companies routinely describe governance, emissions, transition risks and scenario analysis, yet users also need to understand effects on assets, liabilities, earnings, cash flows, investment and accounting estimates. Deloitte (2025) found that 77% of reviewed Financial Times Stock Exchange 100 Index (FTSE 100) reporters discussed potential climate-related financial impacts, but nearly three-quarters were qualitative. KPMG UK (2026) likewise identifies a quantification gap between established TCFD processes and the financial-effects information expected under ISSB-aligned reporting. Academic evidence similarly suggests that quantitative content can be more decision-useful than broad narrative (Bingler et al., 2022; Keeley et al., 2026).

Existing studies have examined TCFD alignment, the determinants of climate reporting, governance, assurance and the consequences of mandatory disclosure (Alqudwa et al., 2025; Gebhardt et al., 2024; Gupta, 2026; Moses et al., 2025). However, a narrower UK gap remains: how far climate-sensitive FTSE 350 firms connect climate matters to recognised financial effects and financial planning, and which organisational characteristics are associated with deeper disclosure. The dissertation addresses this gap through the Climate Financial Connectivity and Quantification Index (CFCQI), a research instrument measuring disclosure depth rather than regulatory compliance or environmental performance.

## 1.4 Research Aim and Objectives

The research aim is to measure and explain variation in the quantification and financial connectivity of climate-related disclosure among climate-sensitive FTSE 350 firms ahead of the transition to UK SRS S2.

- Develop and pilot a transparent CFCQI grounded in current and anticipated financial-effects concepts in IFRS S2/UK SRS S2 and the climate-reporting literature.

- Construct a paired firm-year dataset from two consecutive reporting cycles for 40 climate-sensitive FTSE 350 non-financial firms.

- Describe CFCQI overall, by item, reporting cycle and TCFD sector group.

- Test whether firm size, external sustainability assurance and board-level sustainability/climate oversight are associated with CFCQI after controlling for leverage, profitability, sector and cycle.

- Test within-firm change across the two cycles and evaluate reliability, limitations and practical implications ahead of UK SRS S2 implementation.

## 1.5 Research Questions

1.  RQ1: What is the level and pattern of quantified and financially connected climate disclosure among climate-sensitive FTSE 350 firms across two consecutive reporting cycles?

2.  RQ2: Which firm characteristics – particularly firm size, external sustainability assurance and dedicated board-level sustainability/climate oversight – are associated with higher CFCQI scores after controlling for leverage, profitability, sector and reporting cycle?

3.  RQ3: How does CFCQI change within firms between the two reporting cycles, and which disclosure dimensions show the largest remaining gaps ahead of UK SRS S2 implementation?

## 1.6 Significance and Scope of the Study

The study narrows analysis from broad Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) reporting to the financial depth of climate disclosure. Climate-sensitive non-financial FTSE 350 firms are relevant because transition regulation, physical exposure, carbon costs, asset revaluation and decarbonisation investment can create material financial consequences. Its Business Analytics contribution is the conversion of heterogeneous, unstructured annual-report evidence into reproducible variables with page-level provenance, followed by descriptive, regression, reliability and paired analysis. This creates a transparent measurement and decision-support framework for preparers, boards, assurers, investors and regulators. Higher CFCQI is not treated as better environmental performance or regulatory compliance.

## 1.7 Chapter Summary

This chapter established the regulatory context, the quantification/connectivity problem and the approved research focus. Chapter 2 critically reviews the literature and develops the theoretical and conceptual logic used to explain variation in disclosure depth.
